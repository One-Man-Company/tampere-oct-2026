Two flats, not one

You have Santalahti until 2 Nov. For 2–7 Nov you need a second base, and this time the requirements change: a desk, reliable wifi, parking, a lift (buggy + shopping), a washing machine, and a kitchen.

Best area: Tammela or Kaleva — walkable to the market, the library and Vapriikki, 10 minutes' drive to the flat you have now. Book 5 nights, check out Saturday 7 Nov.

What must be booked now

  • Ferry out, 31 Oct, car + 5. Saturday winter sailings sell out.
  • Ferry back, 7 Nov, car + 5. Book both in one session.
  • Second flat, 2–7 Nov. Five nights, parking, lift.
  • Tallinn hotel, 7 Nov. One night near the Old Town, with parking.
  • Heureka tickets online — €3 cheaper each, and under-5s free.
The one thing that ruins this trip: 31 October is All Saints' Day in Finland, and most grocery chains close or run Sunday hours. You arrive at 17:30 with three hungry children. Buy bread, milk, cheese and breakfast in Tallinn or on the ferry, and treat dinner as a takeaway problem, not a cooking problem.

Ferry with a car, practically

  • Tallink sails from Terminal D in Tallinn; Viking and Eckerö from Terminal A. Do not mix them up at 12:45.
  • Be at the gate 30–45 min before departure; car lanes close earlier than foot passengers.
  • Passports for all five, including the baby. Estonia and Finland are both Schengen, but identity checks happen.
  • Leave the car last — foot passengers board first, and you will sit in the lane with sleeping children.
  • Book a seat or a cabin only if you want one; the 2 h crossing is short and the café deck is enough.
Fuel strategy. Fill up in Lithuania (cheapest), again in Latvia, and once more in Finland before the ferry back. Estonian motorway stations are the most expensive on the route; Finnish stations are cheaper than Estonian ones near the ports. Total fuel for ≈1,950 km at 7 l/100 km: €220–260.

November reality check

Every beach here is a walk, not a swim: water 4–7 °C, air 3–6 °C, sunset 16:15. Cliffs are best in wind and low light; bogs are best in mist. The Curonian Spit road stays open all year and is empty — the best stop on the whole return leg.

Ferries to Saaremaa run all year. Boats to Vilsandi, Abruka and Bengtskär are summer only.

A realistic mix — breakfast and half the dinners at home, lunch out — lands around €95–110 a day for five. That is the single biggest lever on the budget.

Three cheap institutions to build the week around: mustamakkara at Tammela market (€12 for five), a lunch plate at the market hall (€25–40), and the Pyynikki doughnut (€2.50). Between them they cost less than one restaurant dinner and they are the food the children will remember.
Sunday and holiday trap. The market hall is closed on Sundays. Most grocery chains close or shorten hours on All Saints' Day (31 Oct). Alko, the state alcohol shop, stays open. Plan Saturday's dinner before you board the ferry.

If the flat is too loud

Three children in a two-bedroom flat on a wet Tuesday is not a work environment. The escape plan, in order:

  1. Metso library — free, silent, 10 minutes away.
  2. University library Linna — open all day, power at every seat.
  3. Runo — buy two coffees and stay until 16:00.
One honest warning. A working week with three children in a small flat is not a holiday for the adult who is alone with them. Book one paid activity per day (swimming, castle, museum), and accept that two of the five days will collapse. The plan above assumes that.

31 October is All Saints' Day

A public holiday in Finland. Most grocery chains close or run Sunday hours, banks and offices shut, and public transport runs a Sunday timetable. Alko stays open. The evening belongs to candles at the cemetery — the most beautiful thing in this whole plan.

Etiquette and small print

  • Shoes off at the door in Finnish homes and many flats.
  • Sauna: swimsuit or towel, no shoes, children welcome.
  • Tipping is not expected; round up if you like.
  • Prams are left outside cafés — nobody steals them.
  • Quiet in libraries and churches; the Finns mean it.
  • Reflective vest after dark — it is dark at 16:00.

Three things that will go wrong

  1. You will arrive on a public holiday with no food. Most Finnish shops shut on 31 Oct. Buy breakfast in Tallinn or on the ferry, and have a takeaway plan for dinner.
  2. Monday will be dead. Every museum in Tampere closes on Mondays. That is why the plan swims and eats mustamakkara instead — do not fight it.
  3. The 9-month-old will not do three hours in a museum. Split up. One adult takes the big two into Vapriikki, the other walks the baby along the rapids and meets you at the café.
